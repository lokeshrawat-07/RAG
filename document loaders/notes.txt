Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While some climate change is natural, since the mid-20th century, human activities have been the main driver of climate change, primarily due to the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) reports that global surface temperature has increased faster since 1970 than in any other 50-year period over at least the last 2000 years.

Greenhouse gases (GHGs) such as carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and nitrous oxide (N2O) trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere, creating the greenhouse effect. The global average CO2 concentration has risen from about 280 parts per million (ppm) before industrialization to over 420 ppm today. This rapid increase has led to a global average temperature rise of approximately 1.1°C above pre-industrial levels.

The consequences of climate change are wide-ranging and severe. They include more frequent and intense extreme weather events such as hurricanes, droughts, and floods; rising sea levels threatening coastal communities; melting polar ice caps and glaciers; disruption of ecosystems and biodiversity loss; and threats to food and water security for billions of people.

The Paris Agreement, adopted in 2015 by 196 countries, aims to limit global warming to well below 2°C, preferably to 1.5°C, compared to pre-industrial levels. Countries committed to nationally determined contributions (NDCs) outlining their plans to reduce emissions. Renewable energy sources like solar, wind, and hydroelectric power are central to decarbonization strategies. Carbon capture and storage (CCS) technologies are also being developed to remove CO2 directly from the atmosphere.

Individual actions such as reducing meat consumption, using public transport, minimizing air travel, and switching to renewable energy can contribute to reducing carbon footprints. However, systemic change through policy, regulation, and corporate accountability is considered essential to achieving the scale of emission reductions required to avert the worst effects of climate change.
